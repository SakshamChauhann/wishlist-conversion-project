how this works

From unprompted wishlist complaints to a ranked opportunity list

Surveys tell you what people say when you ask a leading question. Reviews, forum posts and complaints tell you what people write unprompted — usually at the exact moment a saved item failed to become a purchase. This engine reads that corpus with one fixed, published taxonomy, so the output is a measurable ranking rather than a set of impressions someone has to take on faith.

STAGE 1

Collect

Public reviews, forum threads and complaints about wishlist / pre-purchase behaviour. Platform, date, source type kept; no user identifiers.

STAGE 2

Filter

Exclusion filter drops delivery, payment, app-bug and rider noise — anything not about the decision to buy a saved item.

STAGE 3

Code

Fixed taxonomy, structured output: theme, blocking strength, segment. Try it yourself on the Taxonomy & live test tab.

STAGE 4

Aggregate

Frequency, severity-weighted volume, co-occurrence and segment cross-tab — computed live from the corpus below, not hand-typed.

STAGE 5

Audit

A stratified 10% sample re-coded blind, agreement rate reported — including where it disagreed.

why an engine, not a read-through

One fixed coding scheme applied to the whole corpus makes theme frequency and co-occurrence measurable and reproducible — by someone who disagrees with the conclusion — instead of a curated pile of quotes chosen to support a pre-existing opinion.

where we were honest about limits

This corpus mixes a small number of real quotes pulled from live web search (Trustpilot, Quora, Reddit-adjacent writing, aggregated app-store analysis — sourced in the deck) with synthetic entries written to mirror patterns those same sources document at scale, since we can't bulk-scrape Myntra's own review firehose from here. Findings are reported as directional, not population estimates. The taxonomy, filter and coder below are built to be pointed at first-party review data unchanged.

Co-occurrence from each entry's primary + secondary theme. Pairs matter: a fit-uncertain item that's also return-apprehensive is a stronger kill on conversion than either alone.

Stage 5 · Audit

A stratified ~10% sample (at least one item per theme) was pulled at random, stripped of its assigned label, and re-coded from scratch against only the taxonomy rules above — then compared back to the original label. This is what makes the theme counts falsifiable rather than asserted.

Honest read of this number: agreement is high here because this is a small, hand-constructed demonstration corpus where each entry was written to carry one clear signal — real user language is messier (mixed themes in one sentence, sarcasm, code-switching between Hindi and English). Treat this as proof the taxonomy is codeable, not proof of what agreement will be on live, unfiltered review data.
